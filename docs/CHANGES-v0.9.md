# v0.9 · Where the flight helps

- **Rapid-climb search.** Braking requests still try gentle 4.0-5.0 s hops first. If none passes, the search tries 2.4-3.8 s climbs at the same 3 m apex under the same 3 g, 35 m/s³, thrust, impulse, 0.70 m clearance and recovery gates. At the 8 m gap this selects a 3.2 s hop (1.95 g, 17.5 m/s³, 59.6 kN·s plus the 12 kN·s reserve, 0.94 m roof clearance) that completes with no modeled contact while ground braking records contact. The 32 m result is unchanged (4.4 s, 82.0 kN·s).
- **Gap sweep (page section 06).** Every initial gap from 4 m to 10 m beyond the braking boundary is replayed with ground braking and with an aerial request. At default assumptions: contact in both at 4-5.5 m, a flight rescue at 6-9.5 m (2.6-3.6 s, 48-67 kN·s), braking sufficient from 10 m. The range, findings text and chart follow the engineering assumptions.
- **Exact braking onset.** The ground replay previously began braking a fraction of a tick early, so contact disagreed with the analytical margin within about 0.2 m of the boundary. Braking now begins exactly one configured latency after onset.
- **Background traffic.** The first ramp car in the exit scenarios spawned on the mainline lane's centreline and was run into in about 6-8 percent of seeds. It no longer spawns; 800 exit runs show zero overlaps.
- **Copy and evidence.** Verdict text no longer refers to an "earlier departure approval" when none existed; the notebook reports a bad staging inset correctly; the highway denial shows its force numbers; the 10 s ceiling is explained against the 6 s impulse limit; the evidence register carries a per-claim source check dated 22 Oct 2026.
- **70 mph.** Braking scenarios accept a travel speed (10-40 m/s). At 70 mph (31.29 m/s): response distance 10.95 m, braking distance 65.27 m, total 76.22 m; lead stop 61.19 m; zero margin at a 15.0 m gap (at 49 mph: 9.72 m). Sweep at 70 mph: contact in both at 4-5.5 m, flight rescue at 6-15 m (2.6-4.0 s, 48-75 kN·s, at least 0.85 m roof clearance), braking sufficient above that. Aerodynamic drag is not modeled at either speed.
- **Tailgating caveat on the page.** The sweep section opens with a notice and its findings convert the rescue window to seconds of following time (0.27-0.43 s at 49 mph, 0.19-0.48 s at 70 mph) against the two-second rule.
- **Replay window.** Replays cover about 3.5 s before the earliest (planned) liftoff to 3.5 s after the latest touchdown (e.g. 1.8-13.3 s for the 32 m case) instead of always 28 s. The simulation horizon is unchanged.
- **Not changed.** B1, vehicle limits, impulse budget, latency, traffic, the highway denial (it remains unconditional because yaw control is absent), and the recreation scenario.
